A working philosophy

Building with curiosity. Building with people in mind.

I’m most engaged when I can follow a question all the way into a working thing. I’ll break a system down, find the part I don’t understand, test an idea, fix what fails, then rebuild with a little more intention.

That instinct connects my interests in code, product decisions, visual design, AI, and research. I want to make technology that can do difficult things and still feel understandable, useful, and humane.

Research lab / questions in progress

Curiosity with a method.

I’m learning how to turn broad interests into answerable questions, careful methods, and honest reports. Status labels matter: ideas are not results.

01 — AI × PRODUCT

Can AI help teams hear users better?

Question
How might feedback tools surface patterns without erasing source context or minority views?
Why it matters
Product choices shape what gets built; noisy summaries can hide who is being left out.
Current work
Scoping a source-linked synthesis interface and a human review workflow.
Status
Exploring; no study run.
Next step
Review literature, define a realistic feedback set, prototype alternatives.
Collaborate
Product discovery, HCI, responsible AI.
02 — EXPLAINABLE AI × FINTECH

Can students question a recommendation?

Question
How do evidence, uncertainty, and trade-off explanations affect evaluation of AI budgeting suggestions?
Why it matters
Money decisions are contextual. A confident interface can overstate what a model knows.
Current work
Proposal and fictional-scenario prototype; compare explanation and reflection patterns.
Status
Proposal; not conducted.
Next step
Instructor feedback and IRB determination before any participant research.
Collaborate
Human–AI interaction, student finance, product design.
03 — AI × DRUG DISCOVERY

What changes when a model knows it may be wrong?

Question
How do scaffold-aware splits and calibrated uncertainty affect prioritization on a public bioactivity benchmark?
Why it matters
Random splits can make generalization look stronger than it is; uncertainty can expose weak evidence.
Current work
Scoping a small, reproducible benchmark using public compound data only.
Status
Research brief; no models or results claimed.
Next step
Select one task, establish baselines, predefine metrics and limitations.
Collaborate
Cheminformatics, ML evaluation, undergraduate research.
04 — HEALTH TECHNOLOGY

How should sensitive information be explained?

Question
What interface patterns help people understand health information while supporting access and informed conversations?
Why it matters
Comprehension, trust, and accessibility matter when information can affect well-being.
Current work
Learning from Voima’s health communication work and exploring human-centered design literature.
Status
Exploring.
Next step
Map user needs and boundaries with guidance from health communication research.
Collaborate
Health equity, accessible design, responsible technology.
05 — SOFTWARE ENGINEERING

How does a small program become maintainable?

Question
Which habits help beginner projects grow in clarity as features and contributors increase?
Why it matters
Working code is a start; tests, structure, docs, and deployment help others keep building.
Current work
Practicing modularity, edge-case tests, and documentation in small games.
Status
Learning through projects.
Next step
Document a refactor and compare the change in testability and readability.
Collaborate
Developer experience, teaching tools, open source.
06 — PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

How do teams decide what deserves to exist?

Question
How can discovery, user evidence, experiments, and metrics help teams prioritize responsibly?
Why it matters
Good execution cannot rescue a product aimed at the wrong problem.
Current work
Connecting product workflows to design and engineering practice.
Status
Learning foundations.
Next step
Write a compact product brief from a user need through measurable outcomes.
Collaborate
Product discovery, user research, early-stage teams.
