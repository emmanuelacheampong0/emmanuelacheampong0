I’m **Emmanuel Sefah Acheampong**, a Ghanaian Computer Science student at **Elon University**, an **Odyssey Scholar** and **Renaissance Scholar**, and a design enthusiast. I’m early in my software journey, with a designer’s instinct to ask how something works—and how it feels to use.

My interests meet where **software engineering, product management, AI, design, health and finance** meet people. This page is my public laboratory: playable programs, visual stories, product case studies, research proposals, and the questions behind them.

### My philosophy

Take something apart. Understand the pieces. Test an idea. Fix what breaks. Rebuild it into something useful. That is how I learn—and how I want to build.

> **Building with curiosity. Building with people in mind.**
>
> Knowledge becomes real when I turn it into something another person can explore, question, or use.

### What drives me

I’m drawn to the space between analytical thinking and creativity: the logic inside a program, the story inside a visual, and the human need behind a product. I care about technology that people can understand and question, health communication that builds understanding, environmental challenges that affect communities, and access to tools that make opportunity more reachable. I want to make things that are technically sound, clear, useful, trustworthy, and humane.

<details>
<summary><strong>Adinkra: why my Ghanaian visual heritage belongs in the story</strong></summary>

Adinkra is an Akan visual tradition in which symbols communicate values, proverbs and cultural knowledge. In my Black Arts Movement project, it connects my Ghanaian perspective with Black artistic self-definition: preserving memory and choosing our own visual language. The exhibition explores **Sankofa** (learning from the past), **Dwennimmen** (humility and strength), **Nkyinkyim** (initiative and adaptability), and **Gye Nyame** (the supremacy of God).

The final collage is my composition, built from transformed reference imagery. Supporting paintings and photographs in the exhibition are labeled as visual references rather than my original artworks.

[Explore the complete artwork and reflection](https://emmanuelacheampong0.github.io/emmanuelacheampong0/projects/black-arts/)

</details>
